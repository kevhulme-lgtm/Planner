1.2.2.12.1 EXPLAIN THE BENEFITS OF USING THE ARITHMETIC MEAN, MEDIAN, AND MODE WHEN REPRESENTING STATISTICAL DATA.

Central Tendency

  1. The Mean, Mode and Median are measures of the Central Tendency, (the centre of the data set). Note: It is good practice to place the values into size order before doing any analysis.

Mean

  1. The arithmetic Mean is the average of the numbers, (a calculated "central" value of a set of numbers).

Add up all the numbers, then divide by how many numbers there are.

There is a formula for us to work out the arithmetic Mean: It means “the sum of the x values, divided by the number of x values”.

Mode

  1. This is the most frequent data value. (The one that appears the most times). Data sets can have more than one mode. It can also be used with qualitative data, such as the colour of cars.

Median

  1. Middle data value, when all data values are placed in order of size, this means half of the data will be smaller than it and half of the data will be larger than it.

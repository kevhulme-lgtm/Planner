1.2.2.1.6 FIND THE HCF AND FACTORISE ALGEBRAIC EXPRESSIONS.

  1. The Highest Common Factor (HCF) of an expression is the largest numerical and algebraic term that is common to all of the terms in the expression. For example:

The 3 is common to both of the terms in the expression: 3x + 3y, so the HCF is 3

Similarly, the ‘2’ and the ‘a’ are common factors in both of the terms in the expression: 6ab + 2ac, so the HCF is 2a.

  1. Finding the HCF is often used as a step in simplifying and solving mathematical problems.

FACTORISATION OF EXPRESSIONS

  1. Factorising is a method of simplifying or tidying-up expressions and equations.

Example 1

2a + 2b can be written as 2(a + b) by bringing the common factor to the front, outside of a bracket.

Similarly: 6xy + 2xz can be written as 2x (3y + z).

  1. In many cases, the HCF is not immediately apparent.

Example 2

36abc – 48bc + 18ac could be factorised to 6c (6ab – 8b + 3a)

  1. In some cases, not all of the terms are common, so an expression can be partially factorised.

Example 3:

3ab2c – 12a2b + d

Notice that 3 and b are common to the first two terms, but they are not in the third term.

So 3ab2c – 12a2b + d would factorise to 3b (a b c – 4a2) + d

  1. Expressions can often be factorised more than once.

Example 4

a x – ay + 2x – 2y

Notice that ‘a’ is common to the first two terms and ‘2’ is common to the second two terms. Factorising for a and for 2 respectively gives:

a (x – y) + 2(x – y)

It is now apparent that (x – y) is common to both of the terms, so it is possible to factorise a second time:

(a + 2) (x – y)

1.2.2.1.7 FACTORISE QUADRATIC EXPRESSIONS.

  1. Factorisation is the reverse of multiplication of brackets.

Recall the method for finding the product of 2 binomial expressions is:

(a + b) (c + d) = ac + ad + bc + bd

  1. Applying this method to binomials of the form (x + c), where c is a constant, gives expressions of the type shown below:

For example:

(x + 2) (x + 3) = x2 + 2x + 3x + 6

And by adding the like terms:

= x2 + 5x + 6

  1. This type of expression is called a Quadratic expression; it contains a squared term, a linear term and a number. Quadratic expressions can be broken down into their constituent factors, through a process called binomial factorisation, which will be useful later for solving quadratic equations.

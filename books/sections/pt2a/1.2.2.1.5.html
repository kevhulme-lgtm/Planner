1.2.2.1.5 SIMPLIFY AND EXPAND ALGEBRAIC BRACKETS.

  1. Brackets are used for convenience in grouping terms together. When removing brackets each term within the bracket is multiplied by the quantity outside the bracket:

Multiply each term inside the bracket by the term outside the bracket

4(a + b) = 4a + 4b

5(2y + 4z) = 10y + 20z

3(p – 2q) = 3p – 6q

a(p – q) = a p – a q

4x(3a + 4b) = 12ax + 16bx

3x(x + 4y) = 3x  x + 3x  4y = 3x2 + 12xy

  1. When a bracket has a minus sign outside of it, the signs of all the terms inside the bracket are changed when the bracket is removed. The reasoning for this rule is the same as the previous section when multiplying positives and negatives:

-2(3a – 4b) = - 6a + 8b

-(p + q) = -p – q

-(a – b) = -a + b

-m(p + 3q) = -m p - 3mq

  1. When simplifying expressions containing brackets, first multiply out the brackets to resolve the signs and then add the like terms together:

(4a + 4b) – (2a + 3b) = 4a + 4b – 2a – 3b = 2a + b

3(4x – 3y) – 2(3x + 5y) = 12x – 9y –6x –10y = 6x –19y

(a + b)(c + d) = ac + ad + b c + bd

(a – 3) (a – 5) = a2 – 3a – 5a + 15 which simplifies to:

= a2 – 8a + 15

(a + 2)2 = (a + 2) (a + 2)

= a2 + 4a + 4

  1. Brackets with 2 terms are called binomial expressions, and brackets with 3 terms are called trinomial etc.

LOWEST COMMON MULTIPLE (LCM)

  1. The Lowest common multiple (LCM) is used extensively in all fields of business, engineering, management etc., to identify pinch-points in manpower or resources.

Example 1

A Land Rover in the Falkland Islands needs servicing every 4 months, a Lorry needs servicing every 6 months, and a Coach needs servicing every 9 months. At what point do they all require servicing at the same time?

The LCM is the smallest number into which each of the given numbers will divide exactly).

4 = 22 6 = 2 × 3 9 = 32

The LCM is the highest power of each of the variables:

22 × 32 = 4 × 9 = 36

So, the pinch-point when all of the vehicles will need servicing at the same time is at the 36-month point.

Example 2

Find the LCM for the following terms:

8a, 24a3, a3, 12a2

The LCM for the above terms is 24a3

This is because 24 is the lowest number into which all of the numbers will divide, and a3 is the lowest term of ‘a’ into which all of the ‘a’ terms will divide.

Example 3

Find the LCM for the following terms:

16abc, 4bc3, 8ac2, 32a2 b

The LCM for the above terms is 32a2 bc3

This is because 32 is the lowest number into which all of the numbers will divide, and a2 bc3 is the lowest of the terms into which all of the a, b and c terms will divide.

In the above examples, it is apparent that all of the numbers are factors of the highest number in the set of terms. If the LCM is not as easily identifiable, a factor tree can be used.

  1. Break down each of the numbers into their prime factors, keeping the lowest prime factor on the left-hand side of the tree. Follow the left side of each tree down and along the bottom to identify the factors:

  1. The Lowest Common Multiple is found by selecting the highest power of each of the terms: The highest power of the 2s is 23, the highest power of the 3s is 31, and the highest power of the 5s is 51, etc. Similarly, select the highest power of each of the letters.

LCM = 23 × 3 × 5 × a1 × b2 × c2 × d3

LCM = 120ab2c2d3

  1. So, 120ab2c2d3 is the lowest number which all of the numbers will divide into exactly.

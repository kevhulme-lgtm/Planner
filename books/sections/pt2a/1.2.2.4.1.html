1.2.2.4.1 PLOT GRAPHS OF FUNCTIONS FROM EQUATIONS AND DATA.

  1. Diagrams are used in everyday life to represent data so that people can understand large quantities of information. The most common form of diagram is the graph. The basic components of graphs are as follows:

SCALE

  1. A scale is the relationship between the units used by a variable and its representation on the graph. The scale on a graph is specific to each situation and it is chosen to illustrate the data clearly. It refers to a set of numbers that indicate certain intervals on a graph used for measurement.

AXES

  1. This is plural for axis. The two perpendicular lines (vertical & horizontal) are each an axis. The horizontal line is always taken to be the ‘x’ axis (Independent variable) and the vertical line is the ‘y’ axis (Dependent variable)

INDEPENDENT VARIABLE

  1. This variable is often denoted by ‘x’ whose variation does not depend on that of another. Time or energy are often selected as an independent variable because other variables can often depend upon the time taken or the energy used. For example: the temperature of melting ice is dependent upon time elapsed, and the heat energy absorbed.

DEPENDENT VARIABLE

  1. This is a variable often denoted by ‘y’ whose value depends on that of another. For example, frictional force depends upon the load in many simple machines, so load would be plotted on the x axis as the independent variable and frictional force would be plotted on the y axis as the dependent variable.

PLOTTING A GRAPH FROM DATA.

  1. Graphs are often plotted from experimental data, to show relationships, patterns and trends. The axes values of the graph are normally governed by the range of the data collected, but they can also be targeted, or focussed to include a specific group of data.

Line of Best Fit

  1. If there is a linear relationship between the x and y values recorded in a study, then a line of best fit can be used. This involves looking for the line that is closest to the majority of the points.

INTERPOLATION

  1. When readings are taken from a graph from between the plotted points making use of the best line, this is known as interpolation of the graph. When the best line method is used, all subsequent readings must come from the line, even if they coincide with the observed values. This has the effect of averaging out errors in the observations

EXTRAPOLATION

  1. When readings are calculated from points not plotted on the graph, this is known as extrapolation. It is important to take care when the graph is extrapolated beyond the scope of the data. Just because a line appears to be straight within the scope of the data, it does not mean it will continue that way outside of the scope of the data.
  2. When plotting a graph, you must always try to ensure that the following points are covered.

a. Draw the graph as large as the paper will conveniently allow.

b. Label each axis clearly.

c. Indicate the scale of each axis.

The Circle

  1. A circle can be defined as the path that would be taken by a point moving a constant distance from a fixed point. The complete path is called the Circumference of the circle. The fixed point is the Centre of the circle and the constant distance is called the Radius, r. The Value of and approximation

  1. The number is what is called by mathematicians, a transcendental number. This means it cannot be written down exactly, except as the sum of an infinite series. In practice, this is not a problem as we can approximate to any number of significant figures we wish to achieve the level of accuracy required for our calculation.
  2. The button on the calculator gives us a value of correct to 10 significant figures; this is more than adequate for our needs.
  3. Often the number 3.142 is used for the value of, this is correct to 4 significant figures.
  4. Sometimes the fraction is convenient, this is correct to 3 significant figures.
  5. It is important, however to realise that any calculations involving multiplying by the number involve an approximation, and we must not assume a level of precision in the answer beyond the level of the original approximation of.
  6. It would be wrong, for example, to use 3.142 as a substitute for and then give an answer correct to 6 or 7 figures. The rule is to round the final answer of any calculation to 1 significant figure less than the least accurate constant used. In this case, only the first 3 figures in the answer have any significance and the rest are meaningless and should be rounded.

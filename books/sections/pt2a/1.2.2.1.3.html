Like/unlike terms
1.2.2.1.3 Explain 'like and unlike' terms in algebraic expressions.

  1. Algebraic terms can be simplified by adding and subtracting the ‘like terms’.

Like terms are just numerical multiples of the same quantity.

For example: 14x, 5x, and –2x, are like terms of ‘x’

Similarly, 7ab2, -2ab2, and 5ab2, are like terms of ab2

  1. The numbers 7, -2 and 5 are called the numerical coefficient of the term and are the numbers that each term is multiplied by.

So 7ab2 = 7  ab2. and -2ab2 = -2  ab2.

  1. If a letter or symbol is squared or cubed, x2 or x3 then compared to x they are not like terms.

So 2x, 3x2, and -6x3, are not like terms.

  1. It is not possible to reduce the expression 3a2 + 2a3 because the powers are different.

The expression is already in its lowest terms.

SIMPLIFYING EXPRESSIONS

  1. An expression consisting of a number of like terms can be reduced to a single term by adding and or subtracting the numerical coefficients. This is called simplifying and expression.

Addition and Subtraction of algebraic terms can only be carried out if the bases are the same and the powers are the same.

7x - 5x + 3x = (7 - 5 + 3) x = 5x

3a2 + 4a2 = (3 + 4) a2 = 7a2

-7r - 4r = (-7 - 4) r = - 11r

b3 - 6b3 = (1 - 6) b3 = - 5b3

Example:

5x + 7y - 4z - 3x + 8z – 2y + 3z

= (5 – 3) x + (7 – 2) y + (- 4 + 8 + 3) z

= 2x + 5y + 7z

Terms without powers are arranged alphabetically

So, 3b – 4c + 6a would be set out as 6a + 3b – 4c

Terms with powers are arranged in descending order of powers, then alphabetically

and, 3x2 + 4y3 + 5x would be set out as 4y3 + 3x2 + 5x

Example:

Simplify the following expression to its simplest form.

3a – 4b + 7a3 + b + 2a

= (3 + 2) a + (- 4 + 1) b + 7a3

= 7a3 + 5a – 3b

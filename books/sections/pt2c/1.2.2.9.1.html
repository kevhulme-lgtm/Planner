What is calculus?

  1. Calculus provides a way of solving many mathematical problems that can’t be solved using algebra alone.
  2. It’s the basis of essential mathematical models in areas such as science, engineering, economics and medicine.
  3. Calculus is used in situations where a quantity is continuously changing, and its rate of change isn’t necessarily constant.

Calculus is all about rates of change.

  1. Calculus can be broadly split into two inter-related processes. Differentiation and Integration.

Differentiation

  1. Differentiation concerns rates of change and the gradients of curves. It is the process of finding a function that outputs the rate of change of one variable with respect to another variable. Equivalently, differentiation finds the slope at any point of the graph of a non-linear function (curve). Differentiation also finds a function which represents a gradient.

Integration

  1. Integration considers the accumulation of quantities and can be used to find areas under curves. An integral is the space under a graph of an equation. It can also be considered as the reverse of differentiation.

This module will look at both areas of calculus.

Differentiating Exponential Functions

  1. If a base raised to a power is plotted on a graph, it will give an exponential curve.

For example, 20 = 1, 21 = 2, 22 = 4, 23 = 8, 24 = 16, 25 = 32 etc.

  1. The graph in figure 7 below represents the functions 2x and 3x, along with their first derivatives.

Fig 7

  1. Notice that the derivative of 2x has a shallower curve, than the function 2x, so it increases at a slower rate than the original function. The derivative of 3x has a steeper curve than the function 3x, so it increases at a faster rate than the original function.
  2. It follows that at some point in between the functions 2x and 3x, the rate of change of the derivative must be identical to the rate of change of the function. This value, 2.71828, is called Euler’s number and is evident in many fields of science and mathematics, such as population growth, radioactive decay, temperature changes, probability, finance, mechanical and electrical circuits and sound.

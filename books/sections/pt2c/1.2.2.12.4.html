Range

  1. The range in statistics is a measurement of the spread, which is calculated by subtracting the lowest number from the highest number in a data set.

For example:

Find the range in the following batch of electrical resistors:

0.93 Ω, 0.97 Ω, 0.94 Ω, 0.92 Ω, 0.95 Ω

Range = 0.97 – 0.92 = 0.05 Ω

  1. The range can be a useful tool for identifying the spread of the data, but it has limited use in statistics, because it only considers the difference between the maximum and minimum values, and it ignores the distribution of values within the data set.

Standard deviation

  1. Standard deviation is the ‘square root of the variance’, and it is a much more useful measure of spread. It shows how far apart the values are from the mean, and therefore it can be used to identify outliers.
  2. Standard deviation is often used in reliability engineering, which helps to predict the service life of an aircraft or aircraft components. The mean value would represent the average failure time of a component.
  3. The mean value and standard deviation are calculated, using mechanical and fatigue testing data, to allow the engineers to establish the reliability and lifespan of the tested components. This data is used to schedule the maintenance and replacement of components. High standard deviation reveals less predictability in failure and hence lower reliability in service.

Normal Distribution of Data

  1. A ‘bell curve’ is used to represent the data from a normal probability distribution. It is centred on the mean value and shows that 68% of all values would be expected to fall within ± one standard deviation from the mean. Similarly, 95% of data would be expected to fall within ± two standard deviations and 99.7% would be expected to fall within ± 3 standard deviations from the mean.

Arithmetic ratios / Non-calc exam
1.2.1.1.13 Manipulate simple arithmetic ratios.

  1. Ratios are evident in all fields of engineering. For example: In a widely used concrete mix, cement, sand and gravel are in the ratio:

1 to 2 to 4 (often written 1 : 2 : 4)

This means that for every 1 part of cement, there are 2 of sand and 4 parts of gravel.

Example 1

4 kg of cement requires 8 kg of sand and 16 kg of gravel.

20 kg of cement requires 40 kg of sand and 80 kg of gravel.

The ratio of sand to cement is always 2 to 1 (twice as much sand as cement), gravel to cement is always 4 to 1.

  1. A ratio compares quantities using the same units - here kilograms. So here 1 tonne of cement would need 2 tonnes of sand and 4 tonnes of gravel.

Example 2

In a scale drawing, using the scale 1:50,

1 cm on the drawing stands for 50 cm on the ground.

37 mm on the drawing stands for 37 × 50 = 1850 mm = 1.85 metres on the ground.

3 metres on the ground, though becomes 3 ÷ 50 = 0.06 m or 60 mm on the drawing.

8.5 metres on the ground becomes 8.5 ÷ 50 = 0.17 m = 170 mm on the drawing.

Example 3

In an epoxy resin adhesive, the ratio of resin to hardener is 5:4.

So, 5 grams of resin requires 4 grams of hardener.

1g of resin requires 4/5 g of hardener.

and 9g of resin requires 9 × 4/5 of hardener.

= 36/5 = 71/5 = 7.2 g of hardener.

Dividing an amount into a given ratio

  1. This involves dividing the amount into equal parts and then distributing the parts according to the given ratio.

Example

Divide £36 in the ratio 5:4

The ratio 5:4 tells us we need 5 + 4 = 9 parts

£36 divided by 9 means that each part is £4

So the distribution is: 5 × £4 : 4 × £4

Or £20 : £16

Check by adding: £20 + £16 = £36
